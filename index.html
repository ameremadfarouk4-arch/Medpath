Anatomy — Anatomical Position & Directional Terms ANAT 101 +

Before reading any anatomical description, you need the standard anatomical position as your reference: body upright, facing forward, arms at the sides with palms facing forward. From this position come the core direction terms — superior/inferior, anterior/posterior, medial/lateral (relative to the midline), and proximal/distal (relative to where a limb attaches to the body). These terms show up in every anatomy topic after this one, so getting comfortable with them early saves a lot of time later.

Physiology — The Principle of Homeostasis PHYS 101 +

Homeostasis is the body's ability to keep its internal environment relatively stable — temperature, blood sugar, pH — despite constant changes around it. The main mechanism behind this is the negative feedback loop: when a variable like temperature rises above its normal range, the body triggers a response that brings it back down (like sweating), and the reverse happens when it drops too low. Almost every system in the body runs on this principle, so understanding it early makes every system after it easier to follow.

Histology — The Four Basic Tissue Types HIST 101 +

The human body is built from just four basic tissue types, and every organ is a combination of them in different proportions: epithelial tissue covers surfaces and lines cavities, connective tissue supports and binds other tissues (like bone and blood), muscle tissue is responsible for movement, and nervous tissue carries signals. When you study any organ later, it helps to ask which of these tissues it's made of — that alone makes its function easier to predict.

Biochemistry — The Four Major Macromolecules BIOC 101 +

The human body depends on four main groups of large molecules: carbohydrates (a fast energy source), lipids (long-term energy storage and cell membrane building blocks), proteins (building tissue and carrying out functions like enzymes), and nucleic acids — DNA and RNA (storing and transmitting genetic information). Every biochemistry topic after this is really a deeper look into one of these four, so keeping this map in mind gives you something to come back to.
